# The Delivery Fee Is Almost as Much as My Order. What Are My Options?

By Seun Gosendeet

You find an affordable item, ask for delivery, and suddenly the total no longer feels affordable. Whether it is a book, an accessory or a small household purchase, the delivery charge can change the buying decision.

**Check whether another delivery method or a different timing requirement would lower the total cost.** Sometimes collection or combining genuine purchases helps. Sometimes the sensible choice is to buy elsewhere or leave the order.

This is also a seller problem: [TheRadar's August 2026 article](https://theradar.ng/lifestylebusiness/your-delivery-fee-is-eating-the-sale-a-guide-for-nigerian-online-sellers) describes the tension between delivery costs and completing online sales. A low product price does not make transport free.

## Find out what the fee includes

Ask whether the amount covers collection and doorstep delivery, an urgent dedicated trip or a standard parcel service. Also check the package size and both addresses used for the quote.

If a seller simply gives you a flat amount, ask whether they can provide the details needed for you to compare alternatives. Some sellers prefer their own courier arrangements; establish that before booking independently.

Do not arrange two collections while trying to save money.

## Compare options without changing the job silently

| Option | When it might work | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Less urgent service | The item is not needed today | Available estimate and collection method |
| Collection point | You can conveniently collect | Opening hours and transport to the point |
| Seller pickup by your chosen courier | Seller agrees and parcel is ready | Who owns the booking and pays |
| Combined order | You genuinely need the extra items | Revised packed size and quote |
| Another seller nearby | The same item is available locally | Product quality and full purchase cost |

Combining parcels only works with an arrangement the provider accepts. Two sellers in different locations are not automatically one pickup, and a larger package may change the shipping price.

## Use total cost, not the smaller-looking fee

Consider a hypothetical ₦5,000 item with ₦3,000 doorstep delivery: the total is ₦8,000. Another shop sells it for ₦6,000 with a ₦1,000 delivery charge: the total is ₦7,000. Those are illustrative numbers, not quoted market rates.

If collecting it yourself costs transport fares, add them. If you would already pass the collection location, your extra expense may be lower. The right comparison reflects your actual day.

Buying another ₦10,000 of goods to qualify for free delivery is not a saving unless you wanted those goods anyway.

## If you are the seller, explain the charge early

Ask for the destination before confirming payment and make the delivery method clear. Offer a genuine alternative if available rather than promising a discount you cannot afford.

If you subsidise shipping, record that expense against the order. [The customer-fee guide](08-delivery-fees-for-customers.md) explains a simple way to calculate it.

## Should I use the cheapest quote immediately?

Check that it includes the handover you need and accepts your item. A lower price that requires an inconvenient collection or unsupported parcel is not a useful saving.
