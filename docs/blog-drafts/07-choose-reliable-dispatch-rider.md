# How Do I Choose a Reliable Dispatch Rider?

By Damilare Gosendeet

A rider says they can collect now. You are busy and want the parcel gone, but you also want it to reach the right person without spending the rest of the day chasing updates.

**Choose a service you can verify and a booking you can trace.** Price and speed matter, but so do the collection record, delivery method and person responsible when something goes wrong.

## Start with the service, not just the rider's number

Check the business name and its official booking and support channels. If someone sends a payment request, verify it through the provider's normal process rather than trusting a profile picture or forwarded message.

Ask how collection is assigned and how you will identify the person collecting your parcel. When they arrive, check the details against the booking. If they do not match, confirm with the provider before handover.

You do not need to publish a rider's identity documents in a group chat. You need the provider to establish that the person is authorised for your collection.

## Ask about the journey you actually need

Give both full addresses, packed weight, dimensions and an accurate item description. Explain any deadline and access issue, such as an office reception that closes early.

Ask whether the parcel will go straight to the receiver or through a facility. Both can be legitimate, but they create different expectations. Also confirm whether the receiver gets doorstep delivery or needs to collect.

A rider with a bike is not automatically suitable for a large box or a delicate item. Ask about parcel acceptance and transport requirements before paying.

## Check what happens when delivery is difficult

Find out how the service handles an unavailable recipient, a missed pickup, a change of address and loss or damage. Request the relevant conditions in writing.

For example, [Gosendeet's terms](https://gosendeet.com/terms) make insurance and claims subject to the selected carrier's conditions. “Insured” should lead to questions about limits, exclusions and documents required, not an assumption that every item is fully covered.

## Use reviews as clues, not proof

Read recent reviews describing similar routes and parcels. Look for concrete details about collection, communication and resolution of problems, rather than relying only on a star rating.

One complaint is not proof that a provider is always unreliable. Equally, a few glowing comments do not establish performance for your address. Use reviews to decide what to ask next.

For regular business use, start with a suitable ordinary order before moving a larger volume or particularly valuable shipment.

## Make handover easy to document

Keep the booking reference, photograph the sealed parcel and record the collection confirmation. Share the tracking instructions with the receiver.

If a collector asks you to cancel the official booking and pay them separately, ask the provider how that affects support and responsibility. Changing the arrangement can remove the record you expected to rely on.
