# How Do I Send a Laptop to Someone in Another State Without Damaging It?

By Damilare Gosendeet

You need to send a laptop to a relative, buyer or colleague in another state. It may be their work device or an expensive purchase, so “put it in a bag and send it” is not much of a plan.

**Confirm that the selected courier accepts the laptop, follow its packaging instructions and keep a record of the device's condition and value.** Good preparation reduces avoidable risk, but no packaging method guarantees an undamaged delivery.

## Describe the device before requesting a quote

Tell the provider that the parcel contains a laptop, whether it is new or used, its declared value and whether it has an installed battery. Mention accessories packed with it.

Do not hide the contents to obtain a cheaper quote. Electronics acceptance can depend on the service and transport method, including battery conditions.

[DHL's electronics guidance](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-us/ship-with-dhl/start-shipping/how-to-ship-electronics) explains that battery-powered items require acceptance and packing checks. It is international guidance, not a blanket rule for Nigerian interstate couriers. Ask your selected provider what applies to this shipment.

## Check the battery and prepare the device

If the battery is swollen, damaged, leaking or suspected to be defective, tell the provider and do not send it as an ordinary parcel. Do not attempt to solve an acceptance issue by dismantling a device without appropriate expertise.

Back up important files and shut down the laptop fully. For a sale, handle your account and personal-data removal before packing. For a repair or temporary loan, agree what access the recipient actually needs; do not put passwords on a note inside the parcel.

Record the model and serial number privately. Photograph the condition, including existing marks, and document the accessories included.

## Use packaging designed to protect electronics

Follow the courier's specific instructions. In general, the laptop needs a rigid outer box, cushioning and protection from moving accessories. A soft laptop sleeve alone is not equivalent to a protective shipping box.

Protect the device and keep the charger from pressing against it. Fill spaces so the contents do not move around, without using packing that strains the screen or hinges. Use the original protective inserts if suitable and intact.

[DHL's electronic-goods packing guide](https://mydhlplus.dhl.com/content/dam/downloads/global/en/packing-with-care/dhl_express_electronics_packing_guide_en.pdf.coredownload.pdf) provides a useful reference for protective materials; the selected provider's current acceptance instructions take priority.

Seal and label the outer parcel as instructed. A fragile sticker does not replace adequate packing.

## Ask what financial protection actually covers

Before paying, check liability limits, exclusions, declared-value requirements and evidence needed for a claim. Ask whether used electronics are covered and whether optional protection is available.

Do not assume “insured delivery” means reimbursement of the full replacement price under every circumstance. [Gosendeet's terms](https://gosendeet.com/terms) link protection and claims to the chosen carrier's conditions.

## Plan a documented handover at both ends

Keep the booking and collection confirmation. Ask the recipient to check the outer packaging on arrival and promptly record any visible damage. Follow the provider's process and reporting deadline if there is a problem.

If this is a sale, agree product payment separately. Parcel tracking does not by itself prove that the device works or replace the buyer's agreed inspection process.
