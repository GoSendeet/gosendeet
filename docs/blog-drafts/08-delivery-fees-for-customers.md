# How Much Should I Charge My Customers for Delivery?

By Ore

A buyer wants your product. You quote delivery, they push back, and you are tempted to reduce the fee just to close the sale. Do that repeatedly without checking your margin and successful orders can quietly become unprofitable ones.

**Start with the actual delivery cost for the order, then decide deliberately how much your business will absorb.** Tell the buyer the delivery charge and service before they pay.

## Separate the courier's charge from your pricing decision

The courier charges for transport. Your business decides whether to pass that amount on, subsidise part of it or include it in a larger offer.

You may also have packaging costs. Keep them visible in your internal calculation rather than pretending that a carrier quote covers everything your business spends.

Use this simple calculation:

**Delivery amount charged to buyer = agreed delivery expense − subsidy paid by your business.**

For illustration, if the delivery quote is ₦3,500 and you choose to absorb ₦1,000, the buyer pays ₦2,500 for delivery. Your business still pays the full ₦3,500. These figures are examples, not market rates.

## Choose a policy you can explain

| Policy | Useful when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Quote each order | Routes and package sizes vary | Waiting until after payment to reveal the fee |
| Fees by area | You have enough real cost history | Outlying addresses or oversized parcels |
| Flat fee | Your margins can absorb variation | Expensive routes erasing the order's profit |
| Free delivery threshold | Larger orders have sufficient margin | The threshold covering revenue but not profit |

A flat fee is a business promise, not proof that all deliveries cost the same. Test it against the routes you actually serve.

## Ask for the address early

Get the city, area and full delivery address before confirming the final total. An estimate based only on a city name can change when the exact destination is known.

State the service clearly: doorstep delivery, collection point, expected window and any agreed pickup conditions. If the quote is time-sensitive, explain when it must be confirmed.

A useful order summary is:

> Items: ₦[amount]. Delivery to [address] by [service]: ₦[amount]. Total: ₦[amount]. Dispatch is planned for [date]; estimated delivery is [window]. Please confirm your address and recipient number before payment.

## Decide what happens after an unsuccessful attempt

Explain your process for an unavailable receiver, incorrect address and a requested address change. Confirm the courier's applicable charges before putting a fixed amount in your shop policy.

Do not automatically blame the customer or deduct an invented redelivery fee. Establish what happened and follow the agreed conditions.

## Record what each order really cost

Keep the quote, final charge, packaging expense and any delivery subsidy together. Review whether certain routes or order sizes consistently lose money.

If a provider adjusts the charge because the parcel was heavier or larger than entered, fix your measuring process. [Gosendeet's terms](https://gosendeet.com/terms) describe carrier charges for shipment discrepancies.

Only when the order's margin can pay for it. Calculate the remaining profit after product costs, payment expenses, packaging and delivery before advertising the offer.
