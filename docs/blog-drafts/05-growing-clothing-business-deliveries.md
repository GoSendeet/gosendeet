# My Clothing Business Is Growing. How Do I Keep Up With Deliveries?

By Ore

When you had three orders a week, arranging each delivery yourself was manageable. Now you are checking sizes, replying to buyers, looking for riders and answering “where is my order?” while trying to prepare the next parcel.

**Give every order the same path from payment to delivery.** A simple order record, a clear dispatch schedule and a tracking update can reduce the back-and-forth before you invest in more complicated systems.

## Move delivery details out of scattered chats

Keep one record per order. Include the customer's name, ordered items and sizes, payment status, address, phone number, delivery deadline and booking reference.

Track separate stages: paid, ready to pack, ready for collection, handed over and delivered. “Booked” should not mean “collected”; that distinction matters when a buyer asks for an update.

You can start with a spreadsheet. Put only the details needed for the task in any shared view and limit access to people handling fulfilment.

## Quote delivery before the buyer pays

A customer who has agreed to a dress price may be surprised by a delivery charge added later. Ask for their destination early, get a current quote and show the combined amount before payment.

Avoid one nationwide fee unless your own cost records support it. Sending one shirt and sending several outfits may create different parcel sizes and charges.

Use [this delivery-fee guide](08-delivery-fees-for-customers.md) to choose a policy that protects your margin without hiding the cost from buyers.

## Set a dispatch routine you can actually meet

Decide when orders must be ready to enter your next dispatch round. Tell customers the dispatch schedule and selected service's estimated delivery window separately.

For example, you might prepare standard orders together each afternoon while handling confirmed urgent orders separately. That is an internal working routine, not a promise that one courier will collect every parcel at that time.

Avoid letting a rider arrive while you are still checking stock or steaming an outfit. Finish the product check and packing before arranging collection.

## Make every parcel easy to identify

Match the garment, size and quantity to the order record. Keep garments clean and protected, choose packaging suitable for the journey and label each parcel distinctly.

Two buyers named Aisha should not have interchangeable packages. Use the order reference alongside the recipient details and recheck the address before handover.

If you pack several orders at once, do a final item-to-label check one parcel at a time. The goal is fewer errors, not simply faster packing.

## Let tracking carry part of the conversation

After confirmed collection, send the booking reference, tracking instructions and estimated window. If there is a delay, share the confirmed status and when you will update the buyer again.

Prepare message templates, but personalise the order details. A copied arrival promise can be wrong for a different route.
