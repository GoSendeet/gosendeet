# My Orders Are Growing. How Do I Stop Spending All Day Arranging Deliveries?

By Ore

More orders should mean more opportunity. Instead, your phone is full of address corrections, collection calls and customers asking whether their purchases have left. You are selling more, but delivery administration has become another full-time job.

**Build a repeatable process and reserve your attention for exceptions.** You do not need to redesign the business at once. Start by identifying which part of arranging delivery consumes the most time.

## Find the repeated task before choosing a tool

For a few working days, record where your delivery time goes: collecting addresses, asking for quotes, packing, finding a collector, answering tracking questions or resolving failed attempts.

Different bottlenecks need different changes. A better booking platform will not fix parcels that are never ready. A new packing assistant will not fix incomplete customer addresses.

Pick one repeated delay and change that step first.

## Give orders a clear owner and next action

Each order needs a person responsible for checking that it is ready and advancing it to the next stage. That can be you initially; the point is to make the responsibility explicit.

Use a shared order record with these stages:

1. Payment and product details confirmed.
2. Address and contact number confirmed.
3. Parcel ready and measured.
4. Delivery booked.
5. Collection confirmed.
6. Delivered, or exception requiring action.

Keep “delivery booked” and “collected” separate. When a customer asks where their item is, you should not need to search five conversations to answer.

## Separate urgent orders from the ordinary queue

Set an internal dispatch routine for orders with flexible deadlines. Quote urgent jobs separately and confirm the selected service before accepting an arrival commitment.

A single rush order should not silently change the promise made to everyone else. Decide who can approve a more expensive service and who pays the difference.

If you delegate, give the person a checklist and a spending rule. They need to know which situations require approval, such as an address change or an unusually valuable parcel.

## Answer ordinary questions once

Prepare short templates for address confirmation, collection confirmation and delay updates. Put the actual reference and delivery window into each message.

Send tracking instructions after collection where available. Avoid telling a buyer “it has gone” when you only created the booking.

Give customers a clear place to request help. Otherwise one enquiry can arrive through Instagram, WhatsApp and a phone call, creating three tasks for the same parcel.

## Keep exceptions in one list

Record the order, problem, owner and next update time. Typical exceptions include a parcel that is not ready, an unreachable recipient and a delivery estimate that has passed.

Deal with the oldest unresolved action first. Do not book a duplicate shipment just because nobody has followed up on the original.

## How will I know the process is improving?

Track minutes spent arranging an order, parcels ready at collection, avoidable second attempts and unresolved enquiries. A shorter routine with fewer mistakes is more useful than simply counting how many bookings you made.
